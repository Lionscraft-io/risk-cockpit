# Partner types — what to ask each one

Reference only. This is the content the **Outreach** tab used to turn into
draft emails; the tab was removed on 2026-08-11 because it was not being
used, and this is what it knew. Nothing here is wired to the app — it is
here so the thinking survives the tool.

The point of the split is that these are genuinely different conversations.
A reinsurer wants to know about trigger design; an operator wants to know
who pays for restoration. Leading with the wrong one wastes the meeting.

---

## Risk carriers  `carrier`

Insurers and reinsurers who can carry the catastrophe layer.

**Why them** — you already price and carry catastrophe risk of exactly this shape — what we are adding is what the capital does after the trigger

**The ask** — a technical read on our trigger design, and an indication of appetite for a small collateralised layer

**What they get** — a live test of response-linked structuring on a bounded, well-instrumented risk, with the modelling and evidence work done alongside you

**Worth asking**

- How do you currently see Sri Lanka / South Asia telecom exposure?
- Where does a response-linked structure create problems for you — reserving, capital treatment, or rating?
- What trigger types would you actually quote on: parametric, modelled loss, or hybrid?
- What minimum size makes a layer worth your underwriting time?
- Who inside your house owns public-sector and infrastructure risk?

---

## Capital providers  `capital`

ILS funds and investors who take collateralised risk positions.

**Why them** — the structure is fully collateralised and the underlying peril is one your models already cover — the novelty is in what the contract obliges, not in the risk

**The ask** — a view on investability, and what you would need to see before an indicative position

**What they get** — early sight of a new instrument class with a clean, bounded first implementation and real telemetry behind the trigger

**Worth asking**

- What is your minimum ticket and what wrapper do you need — note, cat bond lite, or private ILS?
- Does a purpose-linked obligation on the capital break anything in your mandate?
- What collateral and custody arrangement would you require?
- How do you underwrite basis risk on a modelled or parametric trigger?
- What would make this a portfolio diversifier rather than a curiosity?

---

## Donors & foundations  `donor`

Philanthropic and public funders who can sponsor protection.

**Why them** — the people most exposed here are not the people who can pay to transfer the risk — sponsored premium is what closes that gap

**The ask** — grant or premium-support funding for the first facility, and a view on which of your programmes it fits

**What they get** — a protection instrument where the money is tied to delivered response, restoration and recovery, with outcome evidence built into the contract rather than added as reporting

**Worth asking**

- Which of your windows funds premium support versus technical assistance?
- What outcome evidence would you need to see to justify sponsoring a renewal?
- Do you have an existing relationship with the Sri Lankan authorities we should route through?
- What is your timeline from concept note to decision?
- Would you co-fund with another donor or do you need to be sole sponsor?

---

## Development finance  `dfi`

Multilateral banks and development finance institutions.

**Why them** — critical telecom is the dependency behind almost every other emergency function, and it is currently unfunded in most disaster risk financing frameworks

**The ask** — technical partnership, and a route into your disaster risk financing programme for the country

**What they get** — a replicable facility design for an infrastructure class your existing DRF instruments do not reach

**Worth asking**

- Where does telecom sit in your current DRF work for Sri Lanka?
- What is the path from concept to an instrument you could co-finance?
- Do you need a sovereign counterparty, or can this sit with the operator?
- What procurement or eligibility rules would we need to design around?
- Which of your teams — DRF, digital infrastructure, or country office — should own this?

---

## Infrastructure operators  `operator`

Telecom operators whose network is the protected asset.

**Why them** — you already carry the cost of emergency restoration and you already hold the telemetry that proves it — this turns both into a financed, contracted capability

**The ask** — a working session on your restoration cost experience, and access to outage telemetry under NDA

**What they get** — pre-funded emergency response and restoration capacity, priced ahead of the event rather than absorbed on your balance sheet after it

**Worth asking**

- What did the last major event actually cost you in restoration, and over what period?
- Which sites are the critical dependency for emergency services?
- What outage and restoration telemetry do you hold, at what granularity, and who owns it?
- What emergency commitments are you already obliged to meet by licence or regulation?
- Who signs off on a risk transfer arrangement — treasury, network, or the board?

---

## Digital-asset infrastructure  `digital`

Regulated issuance, custody and settlement providers.

**Why them** — we need capital states, claims and settlement to be machine-readable and auditable — not a token for its own sake

**The ask** — an architecture conversation and a view on the regulated issuance path

**What they get** — a real financial instrument with genuine state complexity, rather than another tokenisation demo

**Worth asking**

- What regulated wrapper do you support for a collateralised risk instrument?
- How do you handle conditional release of collateral against off-chain evidence?
- What custody model applies, and under which regulator?
- Can rights and obligations be represented natively, or only referenced?
- What does the audit trail look like to an insurance regulator?

---

## AI & data partners  `data`

Hazard modelling, telemetry, and administration technology.

**Why them** — the trigger is only as good as the data behind it, and the administration cost is what has historically killed granular structures

**The ask** — data access or a modelling collaboration for the Sri Lanka peril set

**What they get** — a defined, fundable use case with a real counterparty rather than a research exercise

**Worth asking**

- What hazard and exposure data do you hold for Sri Lanka, and at what resolution?
- What is your licensing model for use inside a financial trigger?
- Could your data be referenced contractually as the settlement source?
- What is your view on basis risk for this peril set?
- What would a joint validation exercise look like?

---

## Verification & assurance  `verifier`

Independent parties who evidence what was delivered.

**Why them** — if the capital is tied to delivered response, someone independent has to be able to say what was actually delivered

**The ask** — a view on what an assurable evidence standard for emergency response would look like

**What they get** — a chance to define the verification standard for a new instrument class at the point it is being designed

**Worth asking**

- What would you need to attest that emergency connectivity was actually delivered?
- Can telemetry alone carry an assurance opinion, or do you need field evidence?
- What is your turnaround under emergency conditions?
- Have you assured parametric or outcome-linked instruments before?
- What would you refuse to attest to?

---

## Regulators & public bodies  `public`

Authorities whose approval or cooperation the facility needs.

**Why them** — this is a protection arrangement for critical national infrastructure, and we would rather design it with you than present it to you

**The ask** — an early, informal view on the regulatory pathway before we fix the structure

**What they get** — visibility of the design while it can still be shaped, and a facility that strengthens national resilience without public balance-sheet cost

**Worth asking**

- Which regime would this fall under — insurance, capital markets, or neither?
- Is an admitted local wrapper required, or can risk be placed offshore?
- What is your view on parametric settlement under local law?
- Who else in government needs to be in the room?
- What would a pre-approval or no-objection process look like?

---

## Advisors  `advisor`

Individuals who give us counsel — on the structure, the market, or how to run at this.

**Why them** — you have built or judged things like this before, and you will tell us plainly what is wrong with it

**The ask** — a periodic, honest read on the structure and the plan, and a straight answer when we are about to make a mistake

**What they get** — a real seat in how this gets built, while the decisions are still open, and the option to formalise the role if it works

**Worth asking**

- What is the weakest part of this as you see it?
- Where have you seen structures like this fail?
- What would you need to see before you would put your name to it?
- How much time do you actually want to give this, and how often?
- Should this become a formal advisory role, or stay informal?

---

## Network  `network`

Individuals who open a door — to capital, to a counterparty, to a room we cannot reach on our own.

**Why them** — you know the people we need to reach, and you can tell in one conversation whether this is worth their time

**The ask** — an introduction to the two or three names you think are genuinely right, and a straight read on what is weak in the story

**What they get** — early sight of the structure while it can still be shaped, and the option to be involved properly if it goes somewhere

**Worth asking**

- Who are the two or three people you would actually put this in front of?
- What would make you hesitate to make that introduction?
- What do those people need to see before a first meeting is worth their time?
- How do you want to be kept in the loop once an introduction is made?
- Is there a role here you would want for yourself if this progresses?
