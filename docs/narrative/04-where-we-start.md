# Where we start

*Disaster finance, Response-Linked ART, Sri Lanka telecom, and the hierarchy that must not invert.*

> Part of the [Programmable Risk Transfer narrative](README.md). Sections 24–32 of the master brief, unaltered.

# 24. The first application: disaster and resilience finance

The first application is disaster and resilience finance.

This is not the company definition.

It is an unusually strong environment in which to prove the architecture.

Why?

Because disasters create many different forms of harm simultaneously:

- physical damage;
- service interruption;
- isolation;
- liquidity stress;
- loss of communications;
- infrastructure failure;
- inability to respond;
- delayed recovery.

Timing also matters enormously.

A dollar before an event is different from a dollar three months after it.

Connectivity now is different from reimbursement for connectivity later.

Emergency power now is different from money to replace damaged equipment later.

Disaster finance therefore makes the limitation of a single generic payout especially visible.

# 25. First product: Response-Linked ART

Response-Linked ART is the first product expression of the architecture.

It applies programmable risk transfer to catastrophe and critical-infrastructure risk.

It can combine:

- fully collateralised risk transfer;
- multiple capital tranches;
- trigger logic;
- programmed response commitments;
- supplier commitments;
- restoration funding;
- flexible recovery liquidity;
- digital claims and capital-state infrastructure;
- evidence requirements;
- settlement;
- AI-assisted administration;
- and outcome reporting.

The basic proposition is:

> **The capital does not only transfer the catastrophe risk. It is structured to deliver more of the purpose for which that risk was transferred.**

# 26. Respond / Restore / Recover

Within the disaster application, the facility may distinguish different forms of protection over the lifecycle of an emergency.

## Respond

Immediate capability required during the emergency.

Examples:

- emergency connectivity;
- temporary network capacity;
- backup power;
- field services;
- urgent logistics.

## Restore

Capital and services required to bring critical infrastructure back into operation.

## Recover

More flexible capital required for longer-term financial and physical recovery.

These are product/application concepts.

They should not be confused with the general company architecture.

# 27. Anticipatory action

There may also be capital that acts before the worst harm occurs.

For example:

- forecast-based activation;
- anticipatory cash;
- emergency preparation;
- pre-positioning;
- early mobilisation.

This is conceptually important because it demonstrates that capital does not need to wait until suffering is complete before becoming useful.

However, anticipatory action should remain a module or feature of appropriate facilities.

It is not the definition of the company.

# 28. First implementation: telecom infrastructure in Sri Lanka

The first bounded implementation is critical telecom infrastructure in Sri Lanka.

This is useful because telecom is:

- measurable;
- operationally specific;
- essential during emergencies;
- connected to public outcomes;
- supported by rich telemetry;
- exposed to catastrophe risk;
- and dependent on rapid restoration.

Potential operator commitments may include:

- emergency connectivity;
- temporary coverage;
- priority restoration;
- support for critical facilities;
- emergency communications;
- defined service-restoration commitments.

The Sri Lanka pilot provides a controlled environment to test:

- risk design;
- capital structure;
- tranches;
- triggers;
- digital claims;
- obligations;
- evidence;
- supplier commitments;
- settlement;
- administration;
- and reporting.

The correct mental model is:

> **Sri Lanka telecom is the wedge.**
>
> It is not the company.

An internal analogy is Amazon beginning with books.

“Selling books online” accurately described the first implementation. It did not describe the eventual architecture Amazon was building.

# 29. Application hierarchy

Future materials must preserve this hierarchy.

## Level 1 — Core architecture

**Programmable risk transfer**

A new architecture for representing, transferring, distributing, administering and responding to risk through capital.

## Level 2 — First application domain

**Disaster and resilience finance**

## Level 3 — First product

**Response-Linked ART**

## Level 4 — Initial sector

**Critical infrastructure / telecommunications**

## Level 5 — First implementation

**Sri Lanka telecom resilience facility**

Do not reverse this hierarchy.

A previous storytelling problem was starting from Sri Lanka or telecom and trying to reverse-engineer a company thesis from the pilot.

# 30. Other applications

The architecture should ultimately be applicable wherever:

- meaningful risk exists;
- capital can bear that risk;
- the consequences can become increasingly machine-legible;
- and more expressive protection has value.

Potential domains include:

- corporate ILS;
- infrastructure risk;
- cyber risk;
- supply-chain risk;
- climate risk;
- operational resilience;
- energy systems;
- digital infrastructure;
- financial continuity;
- and potentially many other forms of risk transfer.

These should usually be presented as future applicability, not as claims that all these products already exist.

# 31. The long-term vision

The long-term ambition is much larger than disaster finance.

As human activity moves further into computational systems:

- more states become observable;
- more dependencies become explicit;
- more vulnerabilities become machine-legible;
- more risk can potentially be codified;
- more risk can potentially be divided;
- and more risk-bearing relationships can potentially become economically manageable.

Digital assets provide increasingly granular financial form.

AI makes increasingly granular complexity manageable.

Programmable capital allows the financial object itself to become more responsive.

The long-term trajectory is:

**digitise administration**  
→ **codify the instrument**  
→ **discretise risk**  
→ **distribute risk-bearing capacity**  
→ **program protection**  
→ **learn from outcomes**

This is a direction of travel.

It should not be presented as something already completed.

---

# 32. The deepest long-term question

The project ultimately asks:

> **Can we create better infrastructure for distributing uncertain future burdens across society?**

And, more specifically:

> **Can risk flow more easily toward those with capacity to bear it, while the capital carrying that risk becomes increasingly capable of reducing or transforming the harm that would otherwise result?**

That is the heart and soul of the project.
