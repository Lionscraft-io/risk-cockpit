# The deck

*The approved fourteen-slide spine, and what a reader must be able to repeat afterwards.*

> Part of the [Programmable Risk Transfer narrative](README.md). Sections 39–40 of the master brief, unaltered.

---

# 39. The deck storyline

The following is the current approved narrative spine.

It should remain punchy.

The slides should not attempt to contain all the material elsewhere in this document.

## Slide 1 — Risk is the possibility of future suffering

Risk matters because something may happen — and if it happens, harm may follow.

Loss. Damage. Interruption. Instability. Inability to act. Inability to recover.

**Without possible suffering, risk is just uncertainty.**

### Narrative purpose

Establish the human reason risk exists before talking about financial products.

The protagonist of the deck is risk.

---

## Slide 2 — Risk transfer redistributes future burdens

Insurance, reinsurance, ILS and ART are all architectures for moving future burdens.

One party cannot carry a risk alone.  
Another party can bear it for compensation.

**Risk is transferred. Capital is the vehicle. Protection is the purpose.**

### Narrative purpose

Introduce capital as the mechanism that makes intangible future risk transferable.

Do not yet talk about our product.

---

## Slide 3 — The old architecture mostly turns risk into money

The dominant model is simple:

**risk → event → claim → payout**

That is powerful. Money creates certainty and optionality.

But suffering is not always best answered by a general payout after the fact.

### Narrative purpose

Establish the limitation without attacking insurance.

The problem is not that money is useless.

The question is whether the instrument can become more expressive.

---

## Slide 4 — The world is becoming code

Capital is changing.

Digital assets make capital more programmable, divisible, stateful and able to carry rights and obligations.

Human activity is changing too.

Software and AI are moving more processes, infrastructure and decisions into code.

**As life becomes more machine-legible, risk becomes more machine-legible too.**

### Narrative purpose

This is the technological and civilisational turning point.

Do not present blockchain and AI as product features.

Show that both capital and the world in which risk occurs are changing.

---

## Slide 5 — This changes what risk transfer can become

Risk transfer no longer has to end at compensation.

If risk can be represented more precisely, capital can be linked to more precise forms of protection.

The instrument can begin to ask:

**What happened? Who bears it? What capital is attached? What should now happen?**

### Narrative purpose

Convert Slide 4’s technological change into a financial possibility.

This is where the future architecture begins to emerge.

---

## Slide 6 — The new possibility

The next architecture is not only:

**risk → payout**

It is:

**risk → capital → rights → obligations → action → outcome**

The instrument itself can carry more of the purpose of protection.

### Narrative purpose

Give the audience the simplest possible mental model of the new architecture.

This should be highly memorable.

---

## Slide 7 — What we are building

We are building a **programmable risk-transfer operating system**.

It creates and operates risk-transfer facilities as connected financial objects:

**risks, capital pools, triggers, claims, obligations, evidence, settlement and outcomes.**

The output is not an app.

**The output is a working risk-transfer facility.**

### Narrative purpose

This slide must answer:

> WHAT ARE YOU ACTUALLY BUILDING?

No philosophy here.

No vague “ecosystem.”

No generic technology language.

This is the first of the tangible product slides.

---

## Slide 8 — What a facility contains

Each facility has six layers:

1. **Risk** — what exposure is being transferred
2. **Capital** — who backs it, and on what terms
3. **Rights** — who has claims on the capital
4. **Purpose** — what the capital is meant to achieve
5. **Execution** — what payments or obligations activate
6. **Evidence** — what proves state changes and outcomes

**Digital assets represent the financial states. AI helps administer the complexity.**

### Narrative purpose

Make Slide 7 concrete.

The reader should now understand what is inside the thing being built.

Slides 7 and 8 together are the basis for asking for funding, investment, technical cooperation and institutional support.

---

## Slide 9 — What this changes about protection

Protection no longer has to mean:

**You suffered. Here is money.**

It can become:

**This risk has materialised. This capital is attached. These rights change. These obligations activate. This protection begins.**

Capital does not only settle the risk.

**It can help transform what the risk becomes.**

### Narrative purpose

Return from the machinery to the human purpose.

Slides 7–8 showed the thing.

Slide 9 shows why that thing matters.

---

## Slide 10 — First application: disaster and resilience finance

Disaster finance is the first application because the gap is obvious.

A disaster creates many forms of harm:

service failure, isolation, liquidity stress, infrastructure damage, delayed recovery.

A payout helps.

**But it often does not express the protection actually needed.**

### Narrative purpose

Move from universal architecture to first application.

This must clearly feel like an application, not the company definition.

---

## Slide 11 — First product: Response-Linked ART

Response-Linked ART applies the operating system to catastrophe and infrastructure risk.

It combines:

- collateralised risk transfer;
- programmed response commitments;
- restoration funding;
- recovery liquidity;
- digital claim and capital-state infrastructure;
- AI-assisted administration and reporting.

**The capital does not only transfer risk. It is structured to deliver the purpose of the transfer.**

### Narrative purpose

Show what the architecture becomes in disaster finance.

This is the bridge between abstract platform and real financial product.

---

## Slide 12 — First implementation: telecom infrastructure in Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka telecom resilience is the first bounded implementation.

It gives the architecture a real test:

**real risk, real infrastructure, real capital, real counterparties, real outcomes.**

This is not the whole company.

**It is the first wedge.**

### Narrative purpose

Make the product real.

This is analogous to starting with one bounded vertical before expanding the underlying architecture.

---

## Slide 13 — Why this becomes bigger

Disaster finance is the starting point.

The same architecture can apply wherever risk, capital and purpose need to be linked more intelligently:

corporate ILS, infrastructure risk, climate risk, cyber risk, supply-chain risk, operational resilience.

**The category is programmable risk transfer.**

### Narrative purpose

Pull the reader back out from the pilot.

Prevent them from leaving the deck believing the company is simply a Sri Lanka disaster-finance startup.

---

## Slide 14 — How partners can help

We are looking for partners to build and prove the first facilities:

risk carriers, capital providers, donors, infrastructure operators, digital-asset partners, AI and data partners, verifiers, and development-finance institutions.

**Help us build the first programmable risk-transfer facilities.**

Not another insurance app.

**A new operating system for risk-bearing capital.**

### Narrative purpose

Convert understanding into participation.

The reader should know where they fit.

---

# 40. What the reader must remember

At the end of the pack, a reader should be able to explain four things in their own words.

## WHAT are they building?

> A programmable risk-transfer operating system that creates and operates financial facilities connecting risk, capital, rights, obligations, evidence and protection.

## WHY are they building it?

> Because risk-transfer instruments can potentially become more expressive as capital becomes programmable and the world in which risk occurs becomes increasingly machine-legible.

## WHERE do they start?

> Disaster and resilience finance, through Response-Linked ART, beginning with critical telecom infrastructure in Sri Lanka.

## HOW can I help?

> By contributing risk expertise, capital, sponsorship, infrastructure access, technology, data, verification, regulatory capability or institutional support to build and prove the first facilities.
